## Standard and Journal Notes

MinkNote supports two distinct styles of content. The difference: **is time central to this note, or not?**

You can think of these as:

* **Standard notes** — *Evolving* content that develops over time
* **Journal notes** — *Ephemeral* content anchored to a specific moment

Understanding this distinction helps you structure your work from the start.

## Standard Notes

Standard notes are ideas, documents, or reference material that evolve gradually. They represent ongoing thinking rather than a single point in time.

They are:

* Updated periodically
* Organised within folders and hierarchies
* Not tied to a specific date
* Meant to grow, refine, and stabilise

Examples include:

* Personal knowledge pages
* Technical documentation

> For Standard notes, the title is the main identifier. The filename reflects that title, and the content may change repeatedly.

## Journal Notes

Journal notes capture moments. They are snapshots of thinking, anchored to when they were created.

They are:

* Naturally ordered chronologically
* Related to specific dates
* Often short and reflective
* Sometimes later brought into Standard content

Examples include:

* Daily logs, meeting notes
* Journal entries

> When you create a Journal note, the filename is based on the current date and time. This guarantees uniqueness and preserves when it was created. You can still give the note a meaningful heading.

## Choosing the Right Type

Ask yourself:

> Will this note evolve, or is it a snapshot of now?

## Using Both Together

A common workflow is to capture quickly in a Journal note, then move durable ideas into a Standard note.

Journal notes capture experience; Standard notes refine understanding.
